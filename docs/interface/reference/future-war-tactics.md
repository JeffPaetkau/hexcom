# Future War Tactics: SOF vs Alien Invasion — reference file

**Job 5 of 10, pass one (documentary).** QuadCom Interactive / Brightika, Inc., released 17 Jul
2025, "Mixed" at 62% all-time / 40% recent as of this writing (11 Sep 2026). It is the game this
territory picked for one specific thing: "the interface does a solid job in highlighting movement
options and the impact of cover, with clear color coded zones for movement and attack radius" —
one reviewer's own words, and graded tints rather than discrete bands, which is the shape
`conventions.md`'s existing row on this game already names. It is an authority on that one thing
and, by its own reviewers' account, on very little else: shot readouts, ability range, line of
sight, reaction rules and the camera are all reported as thin, missing, or actively frustrating by
the same people praising the colour coding. Treat every claim below accordingly — this file is
thin because the game's own published record is thin, not because the search was shallow.

## 1. Screen furniture

- Squad portraits sit in a vertical column, top-left, one per soldier, each carrying a small
  suit-and-number badge in its corner and a green health bar beneath it; the selected soldier's
  portrait carries a gold border. **verified**, screenshots a87d600e and 5862b7e6.
- "End Turn" sits top-right beside an hourglass icon and a gear (settings) icon. **verified**, all
  battle screenshots.
- A collapsible "Tasks" panel sits directly beneath the End Turn control. Collapsed, it shows only
  its header and a small notepad icon; expanded, it lists the mission's objectives as a checklist
  with per-line icons — "Search the area.", "Eliminate enemy.", "Arrive at the facility grounds.",
  "Continue moving." — with some lines repeating as objectives recur. **verified**, screenshot
  aa445f50 (expanded) against a87d600e and 5862b7e6 (collapsed).
- A circular dial labelled "Rounds left" appears top-centre in one of the five screenshots
  gathered, showing "20" — a mission-level turn-limit readout, separate from End Turn. It does not
  appear in the other four. **verified**, screenshot 4b93b75d; whether it is mission-type-specific
  is a gap, below.
- The equipped item's panel sits bottom-left: item name, remaining uses (e.g. "3/3"), a
  hit-chance-or-damage range (e.g. "45-50"), and a grenade count, above a row of item icons bound
  to letter keys (I, Z, X, C) for switching equipped weapon or ability. **verified**, all battle
  screenshots.
- A contextual ability bar sits bottom-centre, bound to number keys, 2 to 4 icons depending on
  what is equipped: a rifle shows crosshair / move / eye / throw-style icons; a grenade shows a
  shorter, different set. Small numbers appear above some of these icons in one screenshot,
  consistent with a per-ability cost or percentage, but which is a gap below. **verified**,
  screenshots 5862b7e6, 4b93b75d, aa445f50.
- The bottom-right corner carries a single-line, context-sensitive key hint rather than a fixed
  legend: "Space Skip" during a firing animation, "L Highlight the loot boxes" during ordinary
  play. **verified**, all screenshots.
- Off-screen objectives are marked by a yellow diamond-and-chevron icon pinned to the nearest
  screen edge. **verified**, screenshot 4b93b75d.

*What a picture would settle:* whether the rounds-left dial is a permanent fixture or specific to
timed missions — see gap 8.

## 2. The soldier

- Action points are a single pool, spent on "movement, firing guns, and inventory juggling" per
  one demo preview — one AP pocket, not two. **verified**, Tom's Gaming.
- Four fixed classes, one of each required per squad, no repeats: scout (stealth/recon), medic
  (healing/buffs), technician (gadgets), grenadier (explosives). Class abilities are reported to
  offer "some variation," but utility abilities such as drones and airstrikes are single-use per
  mission, which one reviewer questioned the value of bringing at all. **verified**, Video Games
  Made Me.
- Two separate hotbars exist: number keys 1-4 for the contextual ability row, and letter keys
  (I, Z, X, C) for the equipped-item row. **verified**, all battle screenshots.
- Weapons are earned at the end of missions and "add some changes to how you play" without
  changing overall approach; no weapon is reported as strictly superior. **verified**, Video Games
  Made Me; GamerDEAL.
- What a hover over an ability reveals, and what cancel does and how far back it goes: no source
  found. **Gap**, below.

## 3. The target

- Unit health is shown as a name-tagged bar over each figure in the field — ally and enemy alike —
  with a numeric level beside the name and a numeric current value beside the bar (e.g.
  "Ryabo 18 [bar] 12"). **verified**, screenshots 5862b7e6, 4b93b75d.
- A hit-chance-or-damage range appears on the equipped-item panel (e.g. "45-50", "50-110",
  "30-90"). Whether this is the same figure a shot readout shows at the moment of firing, or a
  separate weapon-level statistic shown regardless of the current target, could not be settled
  from these images. **inferred** from the screenshots above; **gap**, below.
- No source found gives a line-by-line breakdown of a shot the way the genre's readouts typically
  do (base chance, cover, distance, each named separately). One review's only description of the
  mechanic is that cover "doesn't obscure you, it only limits your get-hit percentage" — a single
  folded-in number, not an itemised one, as far as any source says. **verified**, Tom's Gaming.
- The same review that praises the movement/attack colour coding reports a specific, structural
  absence here: a 360-degree "sector control" ability whose radius "is never visually displayed,"
  with no way to tell whether a target is in range for most attacks or abilities before
  committing. **verified**, GamerDEAL.

## 4. The tile

- "The interface does a solid job in highlighting movement options and the impact of cover, with
  clear color coded zones for movement and attack radius" — this is the finding the game is in the
  set for, in the reviewer's own words. **verified**, GamerDEAL.
- The same source reports the opposite for anything past movement and basic attack range: no
  visual radius for the 360° sector ability (heading 3), and "no clear indication of soldiers'
  line of sight or even if line of sight is set up properly at all." So the colour-coding is
  confirmed for movement and melee/attack range specifically, and confirmed absent for ability
  range and sight. **verified**, GamerDEAL.
- A grenade's blast radius is drawn as a large translucent red sphere over the ground and any
  structure it overlaps at the moment of throwing, with a dashed line from thrower to target
  marking the arc. **verified**, screenshot aa445f50.
- Whether the colour-coded zones themselves are hex, square, or a continuous graded field could
  not be determined — no screenshot gathered for this file caught a unit mid-move-planning with
  the zone visible; every combat screenshot found shows either an idle frame or a resolved action.
  **Gap**, below, and the most consequential one in this file.
- One screenshot shows a thin ring on the ground around a named unit ("Grantle") alongside its
  nametag; no source explains what it is. **remembered/inferred**; **gap**, below.

## 5. The enemy

- Enemies are drawn in red once spotted — silhouette and outline both — with a name, level and
  health bar over them in the same format as an ally's. **verified**, screenshots 5862b7e6,
  4b93b75d, aa445f50.
- No source found, and no screenshot gathered, shows a suspected-but-unconfirmed or
  remembered-but-not-currently-visible enemy rendered any differently from one presently in sight.
  Whether this game has anything at all for a decaying or coarse contact — the single question
  this whole reference set exists to answer per-game — is unsettled. **Gap**, below, and the one
  this file would most regret leaving unresolved.
- Reinforcements can appear with no forewarning: one detailed review reports enemies spawning on
  the far side of the map mid-mission with no on-screen indication beforehand. **verified**, Video
  Games Made Me.

## 7. Reactions

- Overwatch exists and is named directly, described in one review as preparing "a character to
  shoot anything in their vision that moves on the enemy during their turn" — close to the genre
  default. **verified**, Video Games Made Me.
- The same behaviour is reported as inconsistent by a different reviewer who tracked it across a
  playthrough: sometimes several return-fire shots land in a single enemy turn, sometimes none,
  with "the lack of clear rules or in game explanations for these mechanics" making the outcome
  feel arbitrary. This is one player's account of the experience, not a documented rule, and may
  describe a bug rather than a designed mechanic — flagged rather than trusted as the rule itself.
  **verified**, GamerDEAL.
- Whether a declared reaction's arc or watched zone is drawn at all — the way the tile's movement
  zones are, per heading 4 — is unsettled, and would partly explain the inconsistency above if the
  answer is no. **Gap**, below.

## 8. Camera and input

- The default move command is bound to the right mouse button, not the left — a departure from
  most of the genre's left-click default — per a player's own question asking to swap it: "is
  there any way to change the control of the movement of characters from the right to the left
  mouse button?" **verified**, Steam Community, *помогите с управлением*.
- Keys cannot be rebound at all, per a second player asking how to loot given they "cannot rebind
  keys (which suggs)." **verified**, Steam Community, *how to loot ?*.
- The camera is fixed/isometric with, per one full review, "very little movement" — tied directly
  to a stated in-play cost: it is sometimes hard to tell where enemies are, and there is no special
  camera angle for headshots, critical hits, or utility use, which the same review says makes
  combat feedback feel flat. **verified**, Video Games Made Me.
- Rotation steps, storey/level control, and cycle-target keys: no source found. **Gap**, below.

## 9. Confirmation and refusal

- No in-battle save or checkpoint exists. Missions can run "over an hour," and this single
  omission is independently named as the game's most-cited flaw by three sources: two full reviews
  and a Steam Community reply ("No mid mission (which can take over an hour) saves. Major
  omission."). Read against the genre, this makes any action committed mid-mission irreversible in
  a stronger sense than the usual "no undo on a resolved shot" — the whole session, not just the
  action, is on the line. **verified**, GamerDEAL; Video Games Made Me (implicitly, via "punished
  for anything less than a flawless run"); Steam Community, *Why Mixed reviews?*.
- What takes a second click, what warns before an irreversible move, and what the game simply will
  not let a player do: no source describes a specific confirmation dialogue, warning icon, or hard
  refusal. **Gap**, below.

## 10. What the game hides, and what its players added

- No mods, Steam Workshop items, or community-made fixes were found. Steam Workshop is not listed
  among the game's features, and the most-active community-hub threads are bug reports and control
  questions, not fan patches — consistent with a young, small, mixed-reception release rather than
  one with an established fan-support layer. **verified**, Steam Community discussions listing.
- Localisation is reported by multiple reviews as awkward, with voice acting described as
  "robotic" and sometimes mistimed against on-screen text. The developer's own AI-content
  disclosure states AI is used to process 2D art assets (character avatars, loading screens); nothing
  in that disclosure covers text or voice, so the mistranslation complaints and the AI-art
  disclosure are two separate facts that reviewers may be conflating. **verified**, Steam store
  AI-content disclosure; Video Games Made Me; GamerDEAL.
- One unanswered Steam Community post asks whether this release differs from an earlier title,
  "ССР: Враг из будущего" ("USSR: Enemy from the Future"), which would suggest a rebrand or
  re-release rather than a wholly new build. Unconfirmed by any other source — flagged, not
  asserted. **remembered**, from the forum question itself; not independently verified.

## What transfers

- The colour-coded movement/attack zones, confirmed present, and their absence for ability range
  and line of sight, bears on `conventions.md`'s **Readouts** section — this game is the positive
  case for putting a figure on the thing it describes, and its own reviewers supply the negative
  case in the same sentence.
- The reaction-fire inconsistency, read by a player as arbitrary with "no clear rules," is a
  caution for `briefs.md`'s **Four — who a shot would wake** — a graded reserve that isn't visibly
  earned or visibly watched risks reading exactly this way, bug or not.
- The right-click move default and the total absence of rebinding bear on `conventions.md`'s
  **The camera** section and `briefs.md`'s **Six — the window has a default**, as one more data
  point on what a default bind actually is versus what a player expects.
- No turn-order strip and nothing shown for the enemy's turn is this file's contribution to
  `conventions.md`'s **Turn order and whose go it is** — an empty heading here, which is itself the
  finding: this game has nothing to say on the question that section already answers from the rest
  of the canon.
