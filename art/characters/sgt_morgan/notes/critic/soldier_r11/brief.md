## From the spec: What "perfect" looks like

A viewer comparing the hero frame with the reference sees the same man, in the same pose, wearing the same kit
in the same places at the same sizes and in the same materials, each layer resting on the one beneath it, the
carbine held the way the reference holds it.

## From the spec: Pass criteria

1. The figure's proportions (height, head, shoulder width, limb lengths) are the reference man's, apart from the
   declared differences.
2. The silhouette in the hero view follows the reference's: stance, lean, arms, the line of the carbine.
3. Every item of kit is where the reference puts it, at a plausible real size.
4. Layers stack as real kit does; nothing passes through anything else; nothing floats off the body.

## Questions to score

From the spec's "Critic questions". Score each question listed here from 1 to 10 with the anchors of your instructions, under its id; leave none out and add none.

- **Q1**: Proportions: do the height, head size, shoulder width, torso length and limb lengths read as the man in the reference (allowing the declared differences)?
- **Q2**: Silhouette: does the outline in the hero view match the reference's stance, lean, arm positions and the carbine's line?
- **Q3**: Placement: is each item of kit (gaiter, carrier and its pouches, radio antenna, pauldrons, forearm guards, gloves, belt and leg rigs, knee pads, boots) where the reference puts it, at a believable real size?
- **Q4**: Layering: do the layers stack the way real kit does (skin, shirt, trousers, boots, gloves, then carrier, belt and armour, then the carbine in the hands), with nothing passing through or floating off what is under it?
- **Q5**: Mass: do the bulk of the clothing and kit and the man's build read as the reference's?
- **Q6**: The hold: does the carbine sit in the hands and against the body as the reference shows, as a real hold?

## From the spec: Known failure modes

- Garments passing through each other or the skin where joints bend (elbows, lower back, neck).
- Gear floating off the body or sunk into it; straps that do not reach what they hold.
- The carbine through the arm, the hand or the carrier; a hand that does not close on it.
- Items placed by the drawing where the drawing contradicts itself (the declared differences) judged as defects.
